Foreword

This dossier describes South Africa from the angle of water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is written for the ministries in charge of agriculture, water and trade, the regional directorates of agriculture, cooperatives and producer organisations. It answers three questions: Where is water short, for which crops, and in which regions? It then examines what our products can change, in rainfed as well as irrigated farming, and what that can mean for the country's external trade.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (World Bank, FAO, South African public bodies (Stats SA, Department of Agriculture, revenue service SARS)).

• B: Research institute, laboratory, United Nations agency or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, website, press or estimate.

• "M" means million; "Mm³" means million cubic metres; "t" means tonnes; "I$" means constant 2014-2016 international dollars.

• A "scenario" is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in South Africa.

Key points

• Irrigation, a pillar of production. 2.24 million hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024, or 18% of the 12.41 million hectares of cropland.

• Maize (corn): The main crop. 2,636,250 ha harvested in 2024, for 12.85 million tonnes, or 4.87 t/ha.

• Water is scarce. National water stress reaches 67.6%; at Upington, the air demands 2,021 mm more than it rains.

• In rainfed farming, the gain is counted in tonnes. In the central scenario, 4,168 t of maize and 908 t of soybean more per 10,000 ha treated.

• In irrigation, the water saved enlarges the schemes. 1,519.4 Mm³ a year in the central scenario, enough to irrigate about 271,400 more ha, or 12.1% of the equipped area.

• An organic chain. 15,310 ha of certified organic cropland in 2024: ECOFERT® is EU Bio certified.

• Fertilisers made locally. 640,129 t of nitrogen used in 2024; the country produces 172,469 t and imports 640,129 t.

• Farm trade. $13,192 million of exports and $7,141 million of imports in 2024. First sale: Maize (corn) ($863 million). Rice weighs $641 million of purchases.

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global solar radiation reaches 1,644 to 2,235 kWh/m² a year depending on the region. Energy is not the limit: Water available at the right time makes the harvest.

3.2 Rainfall and evapotranspiration

It rains 892 mm a year at Pietermaritzburg, but only 210 mm at Upington, where reference evapotranspiration reaches 2,231 mm. Rain falls in summer (October to March) in the east and interior, but in winter in the Western Cape, which has a Mediterranean climate. The west and the Northern Cape are arid, and farming there depends on the rivers.

3.3 Climate change

Mean annual temperature is rising. At Johannesburg it went from 14.1 °C in 1981 to 17.0 °C in 2024, a trend of 0.36 °C per decade. At Upington the trend is 0.12 °C per decade, from 20.8 °C to 22.6 °C. Warmer air dries the soil faster between two rains.

4. Water: Resources, irrigated areas, withdrawals

4.1 Resources and withdrawals

Total freshwater withdrawals are 21,110 million m³ a year (2022). Agriculture withdraws 59.4% of it, that is 12,539 Mm³. Internal resources are 44.8 km³ a year and water stress is 67.6%: The resource is already heavily used: Every cubic metre kept in the soil counts.

4.2 Irrigated areas

FAOSTAT counts 2,239,760 hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024. In 2012, the area equipped for irrigation reached 1,670,000 ha, of which 920,000 ha (55%) under sprinkler irrigation, centre pivots included (A11). Irrigation is concentrated on the Orange, Vaal and Olifants rivers and the dams of the Western Cape, and the droughts of recent years showed that water is the first limit of farming there.

4.3 What the water saving represents

In the central scenario, the saving is 1,519.4 million m³ a year, or 12.1% of agricultural withdrawals. At 5,599 m³ per equipped hectare, this water would irrigate about 271,400 more hectares, or 12.1% of today's equipped area.

5. Fertilisers: Quantities, purchases, spending

5.1 Quantities used

In 2024, agricultural use reaches 640,129 tonnes of nitrogen, 490,643 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5) and 312,403 tonnes of potash (K2O). The country produces 172,469 tonnes of nitrogen and 355,000 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5); it imported 640,129 tonnes of nitrogen in the same year. Per hectare of arable land, use is 120.3 kg per hectare.

5.2 Regulatory framework

The 1947 Act provides for the registration of fertilisers with the Department of Agriculture, with regulations on registration and imports (A13). A water-retaining polymer would come under it according to its classification, to be confirmed with the registrar.

5.3 Indicative spending

In 2024, the indicative minimum spending on mineral fertilisers reaches $1,131 million, at international fertiliser prices (calculation in Annex B, lower bound).

8. Extrapolation by crop: Irrigation and rainfed crops

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Irrigation. National agricultural water is shared between irrigated crops (vegetables and melons, potato and sweet potato, vines, citrus, other fruit, sugar cane, wheat and winter barley and other irrigated land), in proportion to their harvested areas; the equipped area is shared the same way.

• Rainfed crops. For crops that have a trial (maize and soybean), the production gain is calculated per 10,000 ha treated, at the national average 2024 yield.

• Each calculation applies the effect of the trial cited: A quarter (cautious), half (central), all of it (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20% of water, with no yield gain.

10.4 Importing water-retention polymers into South Africa

What to know

• Customs duty (subheading 3906.90): The general duty is 0% (free); line 3906.90.30, for liquids and pastes based on hydroxyl acrylic resin, is listed at 15%, which shows the rate depends on the exact line (A12).

• Preferential agreements: The European Union, the United Kingdom, EFTA, SADC, Mercosur and AfCFTA are listed at 0% for the subheading (A12).

• Import VAT: 15%, calculated on the customs value plus 10% for goods from outside the Southern African Customs Union, then plus the customs duty (A12).

• Registration: The 1947 Act provides for the registration of fertilisers with the Department of Agriculture (A13). No text specific to water-retaining agents was found: The classification of a water-retaining polymer (fertiliser, conditioner or other) is to be confirmed with the registrar of the Act.

• Classification: Code 3906.90 is an inference for our products; SARS settles it.

Annex B. Calculation method

1. Agricultural water. Total withdrawals (21,110 Mm³, 2022) × agricultural share (59.40%) = 12,539.3 Mm³.

2. Irrigation. The equipped area (2,239,760 ha) and agricultural water are shared between irrigated groups (vegetables and melons, potato and sweet potato, vines, citrus, other fruit, sugar cane, wheat and winter barley and other irrigated land) in proportion to their harvested areas (2,239,760 ha), without exceeding each group's harvested area. Our sources have no irrigation statistic by crop. Other crops are treated as rainfed and receive no irrigation water in the calculation. The equipped area exceeds the harvested area of the irrigable crops retained: The difference forms an "other irrigated land" group (lucerne, nuts, maize, fodder), with the generic assumption. Maize, soybean, sunflower and barley are otherwise treated as rainfed for the rest of their area.

3. Scenarios. Water saved = group water × trial effect: A quarter (cautious), half (central), all of it (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20%.

4. Extra hectares. Water saved ÷ water per equipped hectare (5,599 m³/ha).

5. Rainfed crops. Gain per 10,000 ha = 10,000 × average 2024 yield (production ÷ harvested area, FAOSTAT) × trial effect on yield (maize 17.1% and soybean 11.3%), with the same quarter, half and whole.

6. Value of water. Three assumed prices (0.10; 0.22 and 0.45 $/m³); no national tariff was adopted.

7. Fertilisers. Minimum spending = nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price (A7).

8. Climate. Rainfall, reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56) and temperature: 1991-2020 means of the ERA5 grid cell containing each city; radiation: PVGIS, 2005-2020. Temperature trends are linear regressions over 1981-2024. ERA5 is a reanalysis: Its rainfall can differ from local station records. On the maps, each province is coloured according to the value of its reference town.

9. Trade. Totals are those of the FAOSTAT crops and livestock line; products are listed one by one, without aggregates. Rice amounts add husked rice, broken rice and milled rice.
