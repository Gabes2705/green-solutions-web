Foreword

This dossier describes Croatia from the angle of water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is written for the ministries in charge of agriculture, water and trade, the regional directorates of agriculture, cooperatives and producer organisations. It answers three questions: Where is water short, for which crops, and in which regions? It then examines what our products can change, in rainfed as well as irrigated farming, and what that can mean for the country's external trade.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (World Bank, FAO, Croatian public bodies (Croatian Bureau of Statistics DZS, Ministry of Agriculture, tax administration) and the European Union).

• B: Research institute, laboratory, United Nations agency or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, website, press or estimate.

• "M" means million; "Mm³" means million cubic metres; "t" means tonnes; "$" means current US dollars.

• A "scenario" is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in Croatia.

Key points

• A rain-fed agriculture. 43,600 ha equipped for irrigation in 2024, for 0.93 million hectares of cropland: Almost every harvest depends on rain.

• Maize (corn): The main crop. 273,160 ha harvested in 2024, for 2.07 million tonnes, or 7.57 t/ha.

• Water is not short over the year, it is short between two rains. National water stress is only 1.5%, but at Šibenik, the air demands 354 mm more than it rains.

• In rainfed farming, the gain is counted in tonnes. In the central scenario, 6,470 t of maize and 1,399 t of soybean more per 10,000 ha treated.

• In irrigation, the water saved enlarges the schemes. 9.4 Mm³ a year in the central scenario, enough to irrigate about 8,062 more ha, or 18.5% of the equipped area.

• An organic chain. 52,500 ha of certified organic cropland in 2024: ECOFERT® is EU Bio certified.

• Fertilisers made locally. 90,886 t of nitrogen used in 2024; the country produces 66,131 t and imports 79,362 t.

• Farm trade. $3,624 million of exports and $6,476 million of imports in 2024. First sale: Maize (corn) ($252 million). First purchase: Cheese from whole cow milk ($241 million).

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global solar radiation reaches 1,318 to 1,596 kWh/m² a year depending on the region. Energy is not the limit: Water available at the right time makes the harvest.

3.2 Rainfall and evapotranspiration

It rains 1,158 mm a year at Rijeka, but only 686 mm at Osijek, where reference evapotranspiration reaches 880 mm. The continental north and east receive their rain in spring and autumn, with hot summers when maize suffers from droughts. The Adriatic coast is Mediterranean: Rain in autumn and winter, a dry summer.

3.3 Climate change

Mean annual temperature is rising. At Osijek it went from 11.0 °C in 1981 to 14.8 °C in 2024, a trend of 0.60 °C per decade. At Split the trend is 0.47 °C per decade, from 14.3 °C to 17.3 °C. Warmer air dries the soil faster between two rains.

4.1 Resources and withdrawals

Total freshwater withdrawals are 679 million m³ a year (2022). Agriculture withdraws 7.5% of it, that is 51 Mm³. Internal resources are 37.7 km³ a year and water stress is 1.5%: The country has water, but little of it is mobilised for crops.

4.2 Irrigated areas

FAOSTAT counts 43,600 hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024. FAOSTAT gives 43,600 ha equipped for irrigation in 2024, 4.7% of the cropland, and 25,000 ha actually irrigated (A6). Irrigation mainly serves vegetables, orchards and the Neretva mandarins; the field crops of Slavonia are almost all rainfed.

4.3 What the water saving represents

In the central scenario, the saving is 9.4 million m³ a year, or 18.5% of agricultural withdrawals. At 1,165 m³ per equipped hectare, this water would irrigate about 8,062 more hectares, or 18.5% of today's equipped area.

5. Fertilisers: Quantities, purchases, spending

5.1 Quantities used

In 2024, agricultural use reaches 90,886 tonnes of nitrogen, 29,042 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5) and 34,958 tonnes of potash (K2O). The country produces 66,131 tonnes of nitrogen and 14,242 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5); it imported 79,362 tonnes of nitrogen in the same year. Per hectare of arable land, use is 181.8 kg per hectare.

5.2 Regulatory framework

A fertilising product can be sold in Croatia under the national regime or with CE marking under Regulation (EU) 2019/1009. For water-retention polymers, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2770 sets biodegradability criteria applicable from 17 October 2028 (A13).

5.3 Indicative spending

In 2024, the indicative minimum spending on mineral fertilisers reaches $114 million, at international fertiliser prices (calculation in Annex B, lower bound).

8. Extrapolation by crop: Irrigation and rainfed crops

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Irrigation. National agricultural water is shared between irrigated crops (vegetables and melons, potato and fruit), in proportion to their harvested areas; the equipped area is shared the same way.

• Rainfed crops. For crops that have a trial (maize and soybean), the production gain is calculated per 10,000 ha treated, at the national average 2024 yield.

• Each calculation applies the effect of the trial cited: A quarter (cautious), half (central), all of it (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20% of water, with no yield gain.

10.4 Importing water-retention polymers into Croatia

What to know

• Customs duty (subheading 3906.90): The European Union common tariff, from 0 to 6.5% depending on the line; the "other" line (3906.90.90) is at 6.5% (A11). Products made in the Union move duty-free.

• VAT (PDV): Standard rate of 25%; the reduced rates of 13% and 5% cover lists of goods set by law (A12). The rate applicable to a water-retaining agent is to be confirmed with the Croatian tax administration.

• Placing on the market: A fertilising product can be sold under the Croatian regime or with CE marking under Regulation (EU) 2019/1009 (A13). For water-retention polymers, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2770 sets biodegradability criteria applicable from 17 October 2028 (A13).

• Classification: Code 3906.90 is an inference for our products; Croatian Customs settles it.

Annex B. Calculation method

1. Agricultural water. Total withdrawals (679 Mm³, 2022) × agricultural share (7.48%) = 50.8 Mm³.

2. Irrigation. The equipped area (43,600 ha) and agricultural water are shared between irrigated groups (vegetables and melons, potato and fruit) in proportion to their harvested areas (31,450 ha), without exceeding each group's harvested area. Our sources have no irrigation statistic by crop. Other crops are treated as rainfed and receive no irrigation water in the calculation. Only vegetables, potato and fruit are treated as irrigable; the equipped area is attributed to them in proportion to their harvested area. Maize, wheat, soybean, sunflower, vines and olives are treated as rainfed, as on most farms. FAOSTAT does not publish Croatia's production value by crop in current US dollars: Values are therefore in current US dollars.

3. Scenarios. Water saved = group water × trial effect: A quarter (cautious), half (central), all of it (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20%.

4. Extra hectares. Water saved ÷ water per equipped hectare (1,165 m³/ha).

5. Rainfed crops. Gain per 10,000 ha = 10,000 × average 2024 yield (production ÷ harvested area, FAOSTAT) × trial effect on yield (maize 17.1% and soybean 11.3%), with the same quarter, half and whole.

6. Value of water. Three assumed prices (0.10; 0.22 and 0.45 $/m³); no national tariff was adopted.

7. Fertilisers. Minimum spending = nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price (A7).

8. Climate. Rainfall, reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56) and temperature: 1991-2020 means of the NASA POWER grid cell containing each city; radiation: PVGIS, 2005-2020. Temperature trends are linear regressions over 1981-2024. NASA POWER combines reanalyses and satellite data on a grid of about 50 km: Two nearby cities can share the same cell, and its rainfall can differ from local station records. On the maps, each county is coloured according to the value of its reference town.

9. Trade. Totals are those of the FAOSTAT crops and livestock line; products are listed one by one, without aggregates. Rice amounts add husked rice, broken rice and milled rice.
