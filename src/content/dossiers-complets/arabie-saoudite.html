Foreword

This dossier describes Saudi Arabia from the angle of water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is written for the ministries in charge of agriculture, water and trade, the regional directorates of agriculture, cooperatives and producer organisations. It answers three questions: Where is water in short supply? For which crops? In which regions? It then examines what difference our products can make, in rainfed as well as irrigated farming, and what that can mean for the country's external trade.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (World Bank, FAO, Saudi public bodies (GASTAT, Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture, Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority)).

• B: Research institute, laboratory, United Nations agency or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, website, press or estimate.

• "M" means million; "Mm³" means million cubic metres; "t" means tonnes; "I$" means constant 2014-2016 international dollars.

• A "scenario" is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in Saudi Arabia.

Key points

• Irrigation, a pillar of production. 3.28 million hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024, or 71% of the 4.59 million hectares of cropland.

• Dates: The main crop. 257,613 ha harvested in 2024, for 1.93 million tonnes, or 7.48 t/ha.

• Water is scarce. National water stress reaches 974.2%; at Madinah, atmospheric water demand exceeds rainfall by 2,752 mm.

• In irrigated farming, the water saved could allow irrigation schemes to be expanded. 2,425.0 Mm³ a year in the central scenario, enough to irrigate about 416,973 more ha, or 12.7% of the equipped area.

• An organic supply chain. 20,064 ha of certified organic cropland in 2024: ECOFERT® is EU Bio certified.

• Fertilisers made locally. 258,330 t of nitrogen used in 2024; the country produces 3,539,966 t and imports 42,837 t.

• Farm trade. $5,719 million of exports and $29,523 million of imports in 2024. Leading export: Refined sugar ($598 million). Rice imports amount to $1,981 million.

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global solar radiation reaches 2,116 to 2,391 kWh/m² a year depending on the region. Energy is not the limiting factor: Water available at the right time determines the harvest.

3.2 Rainfall and evapotranspiration

Annual rainfall is 171 mm at Jazan, but only 19 mm at Tabuk, where reference evapotranspiration reaches 2,181 mm. Rain is scarce and irregular over most of the country: It falls mostly from winter to spring in the north and centre, and in spring and summer in the south-west. Almost all farming therefore depends on irrigation.

3.3 Climate change

Mean annual temperature is rising. At Riyadh it went from 25.5 °C in 1981 to 26.7 °C in 2024, a trend of 0.43 °C per decade. At Abha the trend is 0.26 °C per decade, from 24.6 °C to 25.2 °C. Warmer air dries the soil faster between rainfall events.

4. Water: Resources, irrigated areas, withdrawals

4.1 Resources and withdrawals

Total freshwater withdrawals are 23,380 million m³ a year (2022). Agriculture accounts for 81.6% of these withdrawals, that is 19,070 Mm³. Internal resources are 2.4 km³ a year and water stress is 974.2%: The resource is already heavily used: Every cubic metre kept in the soil counts.

4.2 Irrigated areas

FAOSTAT counts 3,278,920 hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024. Irrigation relies mostly on non-renewable groundwater: According to a 2024 study, it supplies 62.4% of the country's water supply (A11). Every cubic metre kept in the soil therefore counts, and centre pivots and drip irrigation dominate the large farms.

4.3 What the water saving represents

In the central scenario, the saving is 2,425.0 million m³ a year, or 12.7% of agricultural withdrawals. At 5,816 m³ per equipped hectare, this water would irrigate about 416,973 more hectares, or 12.7% of today's equipped area.

5. Fertilisers: Quantities, purchases, spending

5.1 Quantities used

In 2024, agricultural use reaches 258,330 tonnes of nitrogen, 200,959 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5) and 80,535 tonnes of potash (K2O). The country produces 3,539,966 tonnes of nitrogen and 2,345,914 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5); it imported 42,837 tonnes of nitrogen in the same year. Per hectare of arable land, use is 125.5 kg per hectare.

5.2 Regulatory framework

Fertilisers are registered with the Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture (A13, level C). A water-retaining polymer may also require registration, depending on its classification, to be confirmed with the ministry.

5.3 Indicative spending

In 2024, the indicative minimum spending on mineral fertilisers reaches $437 million, at international fertiliser prices (calculation in Annex B, lower bound).

8. Extrapolation by crop: Irrigation and rainfed crops

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Irrigation. National agricultural water is shared between irrigated crops (date palm, wheat and barley, watermelon and melon, potato, vegetables, olive tree, citrus, other fruit, maize and other irrigated land), in proportion to their harvested areas; the equipped area is shared the same way.

• Each calculation applies the effect of the trial cited: A quarter (cautious), half (central) or the full effect (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20% of water, with no yield gain.

10.4 Importing water-retention polymers into Saudi Arabia

What to know

• Customs duty: The Gulf Cooperation Council common external tariff is at least 5% on most goods (A12). The exact rate for subheading 3906.90 was not confirmed in the sources consulted.

• Import VAT: 15% since July 2020 (A12).

• Registration: Fertilisers are registered with the Ministry of Environment, Water and Agriculture (A13, level C). No text specific to water-retaining polymers was found: The classification of a water-retaining polymer is to be confirmed with the ministry.

• Classification: Code 3906.90 is an inference for our products; the customs authority settles it.

Annex B. Calculation method

1. Agricultural water. Total withdrawals (23,380 Mm³, 2022) × agricultural share (81.56%) = 19,069.6 Mm³.

2. Irrigation. The equipped area (3,278,920 ha) and agricultural water are shared between irrigated groups (date palm, wheat and barley, watermelon and melon, potato, vegetables, olive tree, citrus, other fruit, maize and other irrigated land) in proportion to their harvested areas (3,278,920 ha), without exceeding each group's harvested area. Our sources provide no irrigation statistics by crop. Other crops are treated as rainfed and receive no irrigation water in the calculation. The equipped area exceeds the harvested area of the selected irrigable crops: The difference forms an "other irrigated land" group (lucerne and other fodder), with the generic assumption. Sorghum, millet and other cereals are treated as rainfed.

3. Scenarios. Water saved = group water × trial effect: A quarter (cautious), half (central) or the full effect (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20%.

4. Extra hectares. Water saved ÷ water per equipped hectare (5,816 m³/ha).

6. Value of water. Three assumed prices (0.10; 0.22 and 0.45 $/m³); no national tariff was adopted.

7. Fertilisers. Minimum spending = nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price (A7).

8. Climate. Rainfall, reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56) and temperature: 1991-2020 means of the NASA POWER grid cell containing each city; radiation: PVGIS, 2005-2020. Temperature trends are linear regressions over 1981-2024. NASA POWER combines reanalyses and satellite data on a grid of about 50 km: Two nearby cities can share the same cell, and its rainfall can differ from local station records. On the maps, each region is coloured according to the value of its reference town.

9. Trade. Totals are those of the FAOSTAT crops and livestock line; products are listed one by one, without aggregates. Rice amounts add husked rice, broken rice and milled rice.
