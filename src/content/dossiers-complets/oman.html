Foreword

This dossier describes the Sultanate of Oman from the angle of irrigation water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is intended for the authorities responsible for agriculture and water resources, and for the economic players of the sector. It answers three questions: Where is water used, for which crops, and with what reserves? It then examines what our products can change, and what that may be worth.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (FAO, World Bank, National Centre for Statistics and Information, European Commission).

• B: Research institute, university, public body outside statistics, or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, non-independent demonstration, press, or estimate.

• « bn » means billion; « M » means million; « Mm³ » means million cubic metres.

• Oman reports farm areas in feddans (about 0.42 ha). English-language press often calls them « acres ».

• A « scenario » is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in Oman.

What this dossier does not do. It does not turn a trial carried out elsewhere into an Omani result. Water savings and yield gains by crop are scenarios, built from the trials cited.

What remains to be completed. Cultivated area and irrigation volumes by governorate, and the water use of date palms and fodder under Omani conditions. Section 11 gives the details.

Seven key points

1. Agriculture is the main water user. It accounts for 81% of freshwater withdrawals according to the World Bank (2022), and 83 to 92 % of conventional water resources according to national sources.

2. Oman withdraws more water than it renews. Withdrawals reach 117% of internal renewable resources. The ministry's 2013 water balance showed a deficit of about 300 Mm³ per year, covered by over-pumping of aquifers; seawater is advancing into the Al Batinah aquifers.

3. Very little rain, a great deal of sun. From 22 mm per year at Haima to 158 mm at Khasab (1991–2020, ERA5-Land model), while the atmosphere demands about 2,026 mm per year at Nizwa. Every crop depends on irrigation.

4. Fodder and date palms dominate. In 2022, fodder crops covered 42.5% of the cultivated area, fruit 29.4% (mostly date palms) and vegetables 25.0% (NCSI).

5. The potential water saving is large. In the central scenario, 293 Mm³ per year, 22% of agricultural water and close to the 2013 national deficit. The range runs from 147 to 586 Mm³.

6. Date palms carry the result. They give 125 Mm³ of the central saving, using a published trial on date palms in sandy, saline soil in the United Arab Emirates (ICBA), the conditions closest to Oman's.

7. Food security depends on imports. In 2024, Oman imported 898 thousand tonnes of wheat and 325 thousand tonnes of rice. Domestic wheat production was 7.9 thousand tonnes in 2023.

Next steps

• Obtain the cultivated area and irrigation volumes by governorate from the ministry and the NCSI.

• Measure the water use of date palms and fodder under Omani conditions, by governorate.

• Choose two governorates, for example Ad Dakhiliyah (date palms) and Al Batinah (vegetables and fodder), for comparative trials before any extension.

2. Oman in figures

Cropland grew from 72.6 thousand hectares in 2010 to 129.2 thousand in 2024 according to FAOSTAT, mainly through arable land. The area equipped for irrigation follows the same path: Every new hectare is a new demand on groundwater.

Three figures for the irrigated area

FAOSTAT gives 123,030 ha equipped for irrigation and 107,100 ha actually irrigated. The national statistics centre (NCSI) counts 275,909 feddans cultivated in 2022, about 115,882 ha, and the ministry reports 293,000 feddans in 2023. The differences come from definitions and dates. The dossier uses the NCSI crop groups to share the water between crops, because they include fodder, which FAOSTAT does not cover.

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global horizontal irradiation ranges from 2,104 kWh/m² per year at Salalah to 2,307 kWh/m² at Haima (Al Wusta). The whole country receives very strong sunshine: It favours photosynthesis, but also evaporation from soils and crops. The values are those of the main town of each governorate.

In every month and every region, the atmosphere demands far more water than the rain provides: 100 to 250 mm per month against a few millimetres. The gap is filled by irrigation. Each cubic metre kept in the root zone, instead of evaporating or draining deep, reduces the water that must be pumped.

What is missing

The official rainfall normals of the Directorate General of Meteorology were not obtained. The values by main town come from a model with a grid of about 9 km. It smooths mountains and does not capture the khareef drizzle of Dhofar (June to September), which official stations record.

3.3 Climate change

Climate models cited in 2025 by the Carnegie Endowment foresee a temperature rise of 2 to 4 °C by 2050 in Oman, with more irregular and reduced rainfall. Most of the territory already receives less than 100 mm per year. Oman's environmental policy is framed by its nationally determined contribution and its National Strategy for Adaptation and Mitigation to Climate Change (2020–2040).

4. Irrigation water: Volumes, prices, investment, scarcity

Agriculture is by far the main water user in Oman. Most irrigation water is groundwater, pumped from wells or carried by aflaj, the traditional channels that bring water from aquifers and springs to the villages. Surface water is rare and comes in short floods.

According to the ministry, abstraction exceeds natural recharge by 25 to 30 %. The deficit, about 300 Mm³ per year in 2013, is met by drawing down aquifers. On the Al Batinah coast, the falling water table lets seawater move inland: A 2022 model of Wadi Al Jizi projects a further advance of 0.8 km by 2040 if pumping continues at the current rate (A16, level B).

Between the two counts, the number of aflaj recorded rose from 4,112 to 4,198 and the number of active aflaj from 3,017 to 3,206. About a quarter of the aflaj still carry no water. Where a falaj weakens, farms turn to wells, which adds to the pressure on aquifers.

4.5 Scarcity and salinity

Scarcity in Oman is structural rather than seasonal: Aquifers are over-drawn every year. The visible signs are salinity in coastal wells, falling flows in aflaj, and abandoned farms on the Al Batinah coast. Reducing the water applied per tree or per plant, without losing yield, is one of the few levers that act at the farm itself.

Reading by governorate

• Ad Dakhiliyah: First date producer (24% of national output), oases irrigated by aflaj and wells at the foot of the Hajar mountains. The first target for date palm trials.

• North and South Al Batinah: The coastal plain, historically the main farming area: Dates, vegetables, limes, fodder. Groundwater is over-drawn and seawater is advancing: Water savings matter most here.

• North and South Ash Sharqiyah, Adh Dhahirah, Al Buraimi: Interior oases, dates and vegetables, very low rainfall.

• Dhofar: Monsoon climate on the coast, fodder and coconuts; 80% of the national wheat area in 2022/23, in the Najd.

• Muscat, Musandam, Al Wusta: Small farm areas; Al Wusta is the driest governorate.

8. Extrapolation by crop and water savings

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Agricultural water: 1,320 million m³ per year (World Bank, 2022), shared between crop groups according to their share of the cultivated area (NCSI, 2022).

• Water saving: For each crop group, a quarter (cautious), half (central) or all (strong) of the saving measured in the closest trial. Without a trial, 5, 10 or 20 %.

• Yield gain: Same rule, applied to FAOSTAT 2023 average yields and valued at constant 2014–2016 prices.

• Value of water: 0.25, 0.50 or 1.00 US dollar per m³ (section 4.3).

• What the calculation does not contain: No product price, no application cost.

8.6 Reading the results

Key points

• The central saving (293 Mm³ per year) is of the same order as the national water deficit estimated by the ministry in 2013 (about 300 Mm³).

• Date palms and vegetables give most of the result, because published trials exist for them. A trial in Oman on date palms would confirm or correct the main figure.

• Fodder covers the largest area but relies on a generic assumption: A trial on Rhodes grass or alfalfa would change the result strongly.

• The calculation uses an average volume per hectare; date palms and fodder use more water than vegetables, so their real weight is probably higher.

10.4 Water and imports

Imported wheat (898 thousand tonnes in 2024) is more than a hundred times domestic production. Oman also imports 122 thousand tonnes of potatoes and 59 thousand tonnes of tomatoes, crops that it grows but in insufficient quantity. Each cubic metre saved on existing farms can be used to grow more of these products locally.

10.5 Dates

Oman produced 377,066 t of dates in 2022 (NCSI). According to FAOSTAT, it exported 4,557 t and imported 11,005 t in 2024: Most of the harvest is consumed locally or used for processing and animal feed. Higher yields per tree with less water would serve both food security and export ambitions.

11. Limits and points to complete

What remains to be completed

• Areas by governorate: The cultivated area by governorate after the 2012–2013 census was not found; the dossier uses date and wheat data by governorate.

• Water by crop: The water is shared in proportion to area; date palms and fodder probably use more per hectare than vegetables.

• Rainfall: Official normals not obtained; model values do not capture the khareef of Dhofar.

• Water price: No tariff for agricultural water; three reference values are used, the highest close to desalination.

• Feddans: Areas in feddans are converted at 0.42 ha; the conversion matches FAOSTAT for vegetables (29,011 ha against 29,029 ha).

• Fertilisers: International prices, not prices paid in Oman.

• Trials: No trial of our products has yet been carried out in Oman; all results are scenarios.

Annex B

Annex B. Calculation method

Radiation. Sum of the 192 monthly values of horizontal radiation from PVGIS, divided by the number of years (16). Main town of each governorate.

Rainfall and evapotranspiration. Sum of the daily ERA5-Land values from 1991 to 2020 at the main town, divided by 30.

Feddans. One feddan = 0.42 ha. Check: 69,074 feddans of vegetables (NCSI 2022) × 0.42 = 29,011 ha; FAOSTAT gives 29,029 ha for 2023.

Agricultural water. Total withdrawals 2022 (1,634 million m³) × agricultural share (80.8%) = 1,320 million m³.

Water per hectare. Agricultural water ÷ cultivated area 2022 (115,882 ha) = 11,391 m³/ha.

Sharing water between crops. Share of each group in the cultivated area (NCSI 2022: Fodder, fruit, vegetables, field crops; dates from FAOSTAT 2023 within fruit) × agricultural water. The calculation assumes the same water per hectare for all crops.

Water saved. Water used by the group × water saving of the scenario.

Yield gain. Area of the group × FAOSTAT 2023 average yield (t/ha) × yield gain of the scenario.

Value created. Water saved × value of water (0.25, 0.50 or 1.00 $/m³) + yield gain × implicit price (FAOSTAT 2023 value at constant 2014–2016 prices ÷ production).

Scenarios. Cautious: 25 % of the trial effect. Central: 50 %. Strong: Published effect. Crops without a trial: 5, 10 and 20 % water saving, with no yield gain.

Fertiliser spending. Nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price, at World Bank international prices. It is a lower bound.

General limits. National averages; one coefficient per crop group; same water per hectare for all crops; no product cost is included in the calculations of this dossier.
