Foreword

This dossier describes Jordan from the angle of water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is written for the ministries in charge of agriculture, water and trade, the regional directorates of agriculture, cooperatives and producer organisations. It answers three questions: Where is water in short supply? For which crops? In which regions? It then examines what difference our products can make, in rainfed as well as irrigated farming, and what that can mean for the country's external trade.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (World Bank, FAO, Jordanian public bodies (Department of Statistics, Ministry of Agriculture, Ministry of Water and Irrigation)).

• B: Research institute, laboratory, United Nations agency or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, website, press or estimate.

• "M" means million; "Mm³" means million cubic metres; "t" means tonnes; "I$" means constant 2014-2016 international dollars.

• A "scenario" is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in Jordan.

Key points

• Irrigation, a pillar of production. 0.09 million hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024, or 32% of the 0.28 million hectares of cropland.

• Olives: The main crop. 57,840 ha harvested in 2024, for 0.22 million tonnes, or 3.82 t/ha.

• Water is scarce. National water stress reaches 105.2%; at Aqaba, atmospheric water demand exceeds rainfall by 1,971 mm.

• In irrigated farming, the water saved could allow irrigation schemes to be expanded. 96.5 Mm³ a year in the central scenario, enough to irrigate about 17,947 more ha, or 19.7% of the equipped area.

• An organic supply chain. 580 ha of certified organic cropland in 2024: ECOFERT® is EU Bio certified.

• Fertilisers made locally. 16,039 t of nitrogen used in 2024; the country produces 151,100 t and imports 27,264 t.

• Farm trade. $1,018 million of exports and $4,533 million of imports in 2024. Leading export: Sheep ($135 million). Rice imports amount to $232 million.

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global solar radiation reaches 1,999 to 2,290 kWh/m² a year depending on the region. Energy is not the limiting factor: Water available at the right time determines the harvest.

3.2 Rainfall and evapotranspiration

Annual rainfall is 373 mm at Irbid, but only 47 mm at Aqaba, where reference evapotranspiration reaches 2,018 mm. Rain falls from October to April, mostly from December to February, and the summer is completely dry. The north-western heights receive the most water; the east and south are desert.

3.3 Climate change

Mean annual temperature is rising. At Amman it went from 16.6 °C in 1981 to 18.8 °C in 2024, a trend of 0.51 °C per decade. At Deir Alla the trend is 0.49 °C per decade, from 20.1 °C to 22.3 °C. Warmer air dries the soil faster between rainfall events.

4. Water: Resources, irrigated areas, withdrawals

4.1 Resources and withdrawals

Total freshwater withdrawals are 950 million m³ a year (2022). Agriculture accounts for 51.6% of these withdrawals, that is 491 Mm³. Internal resources are 0.7 km³ a year and water stress is 105.2%: The resource is already heavily used: Every cubic metre kept in the soil counts.

4.2 Irrigated areas

FAOSTAT counts 91,239 hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024. In 2024, the area equipped for irrigation reaches 91,240 ha and the area actually irrigated 92,800 ha; 4,300 ha of crops are under protective cover (A6). Irrigation is concentrated in the Jordan Valley, fed by the King Abdullah Canal, and on borehole farms of the eastern and southern plateaus.

4.3 What the water saving represents

In the central scenario, the saving is 96.5 million m³ a year, or 19.7% of agricultural withdrawals. At 5,377 m³ per equipped hectare, this water would irrigate about 17,947 more hectares, or 19.7% of today's equipped area.

5. Fertilisers: Quantities, purchases, spending

5.1 Quantities used

In 2024, agricultural use reaches 16,039 tonnes of nitrogen, 2,945 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5) and 3,515 tonnes of potash (K2O). The country produces 151,100 tonnes of nitrogen and 383,400 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5); it imported 27,264 tonnes of nitrogen in the same year. Per hectare of arable land, use is 111.9 kg per hectare.

5.2 Regulatory framework

Agriculture Law No. 13 of 2015 requires a licence to produce and trade fertilisers, from the Ministry of Agriculture (A13). A water-retaining polymer may also require registration, depending on its classification, to be confirmed with the ministry.

5.3 Indicative spending

In 2024, the indicative minimum spending on mineral fertilisers reaches $17 million, at international fertiliser prices (calculation in Annex B, lower bound).

8. Extrapolation by crop: Irrigation and rainfed crops

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Irrigation. National agricultural water is shared between irrigated crops (vegetables and melons, potato, date palm, citrus, other fruit and other irrigated land), in proportion to their harvested areas; the equipped area is shared the same way.

• Each calculation applies the effect of the trial cited: A quarter (cautious), half (central) or the full effect (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20% of water, with no yield gain.

The three main farm products exported in 2024 are: Sheep ($135 million), peaches and nectarines ($63 million) and other manufactured tobacco ($47 million).

10.3 Imports

The main farm purchases are: Meat of chickens, fresh or chilled ($183 million), unmanufactured tobacco ($169 million) and sheep ($162 million). Rice imports amount to $232 million (husked, broken and milled rice): Every tonne grown locally reduces this bill.

10.4 Importing water-retention polymers into Jordan

What to know

• Customs duty (subheading 3906.90): 0% at the most-favoured-nation rate, on the only national line of the subheading (A11).

• General sales tax: 16%, a tax that works like a VAT (A12).

• Licence: Law No. 13 of 2015 requires a licence to produce and trade fertilisers and plant growth regulators, from the Ministry of Agriculture (A13). No text specific to water-retaining agents was found: Their classification (fertiliser, soil conditioner or other) is to be confirmed with the ministry.

• Classification: Code 3906.90 is an inference for our products; Jordan Customs settles it.

Annex B. Calculation method

1. Agricultural water. Total withdrawals (950 Mm³, 2022) × agricultural share (51.65%) = 490.6 Mm³.

2. Irrigation. The equipped area (91,239 ha) and agricultural water are shared between irrigated groups (vegetables and melons, potato, date palm, citrus, other fruit and other irrigated land) in proportion to their harvested areas (91,239 ha), without exceeding each group's harvested area. Our sources provide no irrigation statistics by crop. Other crops are treated as rainfed and receive no irrigation water in the calculation. The equipped area exceeds the harvested area of the selected irrigable crops: The difference forms an "other irrigated land" group (irrigated olives, fodder, irrigated cereals), with the generic assumption. Olives and cereals are otherwise treated as rainfed for the rest of their area.

3. Scenarios. Water saved = group water × trial effect: A quarter (cautious), half (central) or the full effect (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20%.

4. Extra hectares. Water saved ÷ water per equipped hectare (5,377 m³/ha).

6. Value of water. Three assumed prices (0.10; 0.22 and 0.45 $/m³); no national tariff was adopted.

7. Fertilisers. Minimum spending = nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price (A7).

8. Climate. Rainfall, reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56) and temperature: 1991-2020 means of the NASA POWER grid cell containing each city; radiation: PVGIS, 2005-2020. Temperature trends are linear regressions over 1981-2024. NASA POWER combines reanalyses and satellite data on a grid of about 50 km: Two nearby cities can share the same cell, and its rainfall can differ from local station records. On the maps, each governorate is coloured according to the value of its reference town.

9. Trade. Totals are those of the FAOSTAT crops and livestock line; products are listed one by one, without aggregates. Rice amounts add husked rice, broken rice and milled rice.
