Foreword

This dossier describes Greece from the angle of water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is written for the ministries in charge of agriculture, water and trade, the regional directorates of agriculture, cooperatives and producer organisations. It answers three questions: Where is water in short supply? For which crops? In which regions? It then examines what difference our products can make, in rainfed as well as irrigated farming, and what that can mean for the country's external trade.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (World Bank, FAO, Greek public bodies (ELSTAT, Ministry of Rural Development and Food, AADE tax authority) and the European Union).

• B: Research institute, laboratory, United Nations agency or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, website, press or estimate.

• "M" means million; "Mm³" means million cubic metres; "t" means tonnes; "$" means current US dollars.

• A "scenario" is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in Greece.

Key points

• Irrigation, a pillar of production. 2.00 million hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024, or 69% of the 2.89 million hectares of cropland.

• Olives: The main crop. 840,620 ha harvested in 2023, for 2.50 million tonnes, or 2.97 t/ha.

• Water is not scarce over the year as a whole; it is scarce between rainfall events. National water stress is only 20.3%, but at Heraklion, atmospheric water demand exceeds rainfall by 889 mm.

• In irrigated farming, the water saved could allow irrigation schemes to be expanded. 906.0 Mm³ a year in the central scenario, enough to irrigate about 224,226 more ha, or 11.2% of the equipped area.

• An organic supply chain. 271,690 ha of certified organic cropland in 2024: ECOFERT® is EU Bio certified.

• All nitrogen is imported. 195,817 t of nitrogen used in 2024, 222,370 t imported, no national production.

• Farm trade. $10,625 million of exports and $10,835 million of imports in 2024. Leading export: Cheese made from whole cow's milk ($1,055 million). Leading import: Cheese made from whole cow's milk ($755 million).

2. Greece in figures

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global solar radiation reaches 1,584 to 1,882 kWh/m² a year depending on the region. Energy is not the limiting factor: Water available at the right time determines the harvest.

3.2 Rainfall and evapotranspiration

Annual rainfall is 1,020 mm at Corfu, but only 442 mm at Naxos, where reference evapotranspiration reaches 1,278 mm. Rain falls from October to March, heavier in the west (Epirus, Ionian Islands) than in the east and the Cyclades. Summer is hot and dry: It is the season when irrigated crops need water most.

3.3 Climate change

Mean annual temperature is rising. At Larissa it went from 15.4 °C in 1981 to 18.2 °C in 2024, a trend of 0.48 °C per decade. At Heraklion the trend is 0.36 °C per decade, from 18.7 °C to 20.4 °C. Warmer air dries the soil faster between rainfall events.

4. Water: Resources, irrigated areas, withdrawals

4.1 Resources and withdrawals

Total freshwater withdrawals are 10,047 million m³ a year (2022). Agriculture accounts for 80.5% of these withdrawals, that is 8,085 Mm³. Internal resources are 58.0 km³ a year and water stress is 20.3%: The country has water, but little of it is mobilised for crops.

4.2 Irrigated areas

FAOSTAT gives 2,001,000 ha equipped for irrigation in 2024, a figure unchanged for several years, and 1,175,870 ha actually irrigated in 2024 (A6). Irrigation dominates on the plains of Thessaly, Macedonia and Thrace (cotton, maize, rice, fruit) and in Crete (greenhouse vegetables, olives).

4.3 What the water saving represents

In the central scenario, the saving is 906.0 million m³ a year, or 11.2% of agricultural withdrawals. At 4,041 m³ per equipped hectare, this water would irrigate about 224,226 more hectares, or 11.2% of today's equipped area.

5. Fertilisers: Quantities, purchases, spending

5.1 Quantities used

In 2024, agricultural use reaches 195,817 tonnes of nitrogen, 59,864 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5) and 86,724 tonnes of potash (K2O). The country produces no nitrogen fertiliser: It imported 222,370 tonnes of nitrogen in the same year. Per hectare of arable land, use is 186.6 kg per hectare.

5.2 Regulatory framework

A fertilising product can be sold in Greece under the national regime or with CE marking under Regulation (EU) 2019/1009. For water-retention polymers, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2770 sets biodegradability criteria applicable from 17 October 2028 (A13).

5.3 Indicative spending

In 2024, the indicative minimum spending on mineral fertilisers reaches $248 million, at international fertiliser prices (calculation in Annex B, lower bound).

8. Extrapolation by crop: Irrigation and rainfed crops

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Irrigation. National agricultural water is shared between irrigated crops (vegetables and melons, potato, maize, cotton, rice, fruit, vines and other irrigated land), in proportion to their harvested areas; the equipped area is shared the same way.

• Each calculation applies the effect of the trial cited: A quarter (cautious), half (central) or the full effect (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20% of water, with no yield gain.

10.4 Importing water-retention polymers into Greece

What to know

• Customs duty (subheading 3906.90): The European Union common tariff, from 0 to 6.5% depending on the line; the "other" line (3906.90.90) is at 6.5% (A11). Products made in the Union move duty-free.

• VAT: Standard rate of 24%; the reduced rates of 13% and 6% cover lists of goods set by law (A12). The rate applicable to a water-retaining agent is to be confirmed with the AADE tax authority.

• Placing on the market: A fertilising product can be sold under the Greek regime or with CE marking under Regulation (EU) 2019/1009 (A13). For water-retention polymers, Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/2770 sets biodegradability criteria applicable from 17 October 2028 (A13).

• Classification: Code 3906.90 is an inference for our products; Greek Customs settles it.

Annex B. Calculation method

1. Agricultural water. Total withdrawals (10,047 Mm³, 2022) × agricultural share (80.47%) = 8,085.3 Mm³.

2. Irrigation. The equipped area (2,001,000 ha) and agricultural water are shared between irrigated groups (vegetables and melons, potato, maize, cotton, rice, fruit, vines and other irrigated land) in proportion to their harvested areas (2,001,000 ha), without exceeding each group's harvested area. Our sources provide no irrigation statistics by crop. Other crops are treated as rainfed and receive no irrigation water in the calculation. The crop data used are those of 2023: For 2024, FAOSTAT does not yet give the cotton or olive harvest. The equipped area exceeds the harvested area of the selected irrigable crops: The difference forms an "other irrigated land" group (irrigated olives and vines, lucerne, fodder), with the generic assumption. Maize is treated as irrigated, as on the Greek plains. FAOSTAT does not publish Greece's production value by crop in international dollars at constant prices: Values are therefore in current US dollars.

3. Scenarios. Water saved = group water × trial effect: A quarter (cautious), half (central) or the full effect (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20%.

4. Extra hectares. Water saved ÷ water per equipped hectare (4,041 m³/ha).

6. Value of water. Three assumed prices (0.10; 0.22 and 0.45 $/m³); no national tariff was adopted.

7. Fertilisers. Minimum spending = nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price (A7).

8. Climate. Rainfall, reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56) and temperature: 1991-2020 means of the NASA POWER grid cell containing each city; radiation: PVGIS, 2005-2020. Temperature trends are linear regressions over 1981-2024. NASA POWER combines reanalyses and satellite data on a grid of about 50 km: Two nearby cities can share the same cell, and its rainfall can differ from local station records. On the maps, each region is coloured according to the value of its reference town.

9. Trade. Totals are those of the FAOSTAT crops and livestock line; products are listed one by one, without aggregates. Rice amounts add husked rice, broken rice and milled rice.
