Foreword

This dossier describes Egypt from the angle of water, climate, fertilisers and crops. It is written for the ministries in charge of agriculture, water and trade, the regional directorates of agriculture, cooperatives and producer organisations. It answers three questions: Where is water short, for which crops, and in which regions? It then examines what our products can change, in rainfed as well as irrigated farming, and what that can mean for the country's external trade.

Reading the sources. Every figure refers to a source, named in the text and detailed in Annex A. Each source carries a confidence level:

How to read this document

• A: Official statistics or international database (World Bank, FAO, Egyptian public bodies (CAPMAS, Ministry of Agriculture and Land Reclamation)).

• B: Research institute, laboratory, United Nations agency or recognised climate model.

• C: Manufacturer statement, website, press or estimate.

• "M" means million; "Mm³" means million cubic metres; "t" means tonnes; "I$" means constant 2014-2016 international dollars.

• A "scenario" is a calculation based on the trials cited. It is not a measurement made in Egypt.

Key points

• Irrigation, a pillar of production. 4.01 million hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024, or 95% of the 4.23 million hectares of cropland.

• Wheat: The main crop. 1,386,892 ha harvested in 2024, for 9.40 million tonnes, or 6.78 t/ha.

• An organic chain. 115,490 ha of certified organic cropland in 2024: ECOFERT® is EU Bio certified.

• Farm trade. $10,014 million of exports and $18,981 million of imports in 2024. First sale: Oranges ($893 million). First purchase: Wheat ($4,443 million).

2. Egypt in figures

Egypt had 118.37 million inhabitants in 2025, 57% of them in rural areas. Agriculture accounts for 16.6% of gross domestic product and employs 18% of the labour force. The country covers 995.5 thousand km², of which 4% is agricultural land. Undernourishment affects 8.4% of the population.

3. Sunshine, rainfall and climate change

3.1 Sunshine

Global solar radiation reaches 2,101 to 2,440 kWh/m² a year depending on the region. Energy is not the limit: Water available at the right time makes the harvest.

3.2 Rainfall and evapotranspiration

It rains 95 mm a year at Alexandrie, but only 2 mm at Assouan, where reference evapotranspiration reaches 2,940 mm. Rain is almost limited to the north coast, between autumn and spring; further south it is negligible and farming lives on irrigation. Summer is hot everywhere and the heat of Upper Egypt is the strongest.

3.3 Climate change

Mean annual temperature is rising. At Le Caire it went from 21.3 °C in 1981 to 24.2 °C in 2024, a trend of 0.59 °C per decade. At Assouan the trend is 0.39 °C per decade, from 25.9 °C to 27.5 °C. Warmer air dries the soil faster between two rains.

4. Water: Resources, irrigated areas, withdrawals

4.1 Resources and withdrawals

Total freshwater withdrawals are 77,500 million m³ a year (2022). Agriculture withdraws 79.2% of it, that is 61,350 Mm³. Internal resources are 1.0 km³ a year and water stress is 141.2%: The resource is already heavily used: Every cubic metre kept in the soil counts.

4.2 Irrigated areas

FAOSTAT counts 4,013,710 hectares equipped for irrigation in 2024. FAO estimated the irrigation potential at 4,435,000 ha in 1993, for 3,246,000 ha equipped, of which 3,046,000 ha under surface irrigation (A11). Egypt receives 55.5 km³ a year from the Nile under the 1959 agreement with Sudan (A11). Water is therefore a quantity fixed by treaty: Hectares won from the desert are paid for with water saved elsewhere.

4.3 What the water saving represents

In the central scenario, the saving is 9,469.0 million m³ a year, or 15.4% of agricultural withdrawals. At 15,285 m³ per equipped hectare, this water would irrigate about 619,489 more hectares, or 15.4% of today's equipped area.

5. Fertilisers: Quantities, purchases, spending

5.1 Quantities used

In 2024, agricultural use reaches 1,586,809 tonnes of nitrogen, 230,114 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5) and 140,200 tonnes of potash (K2O). The country produces 3,198,000 tonnes of nitrogen and 230,114 tonnes of phosphate (P2O5); it imported 23,852 tonnes of nitrogen in the same year. Per hectare of arable land, use is 597.8 kg per hectare.

5.2 Regulatory framework

A fertiliser and soil conditioner control department exists at the Soil, Water and Environment Research Institute, and the Egyptian standards body publishes standards in the field (A13). The exact registration procedure for a water-retaining polymer was not found.

5.3 Indicative spending

In 2024, the indicative minimum spending on mineral fertilisers reaches $1,473 million, at international fertiliser prices (calculation in Annex B, lower bound).

8. Extrapolation by crop: Irrigation and rainfed crops

8.1 Method

Method in brief

• Irrigation. National agricultural water is shared between irrigated crops (wheat, barley, sorghum, maize, rice, sugar cane and sugar beet, potato, vegetables and melons, citrus, date palm, other fruit, cotton, groundnut, sesame, sunflower, soybean and pulses), in proportion to their harvested areas; the equipped area is shared the same way.

• Each calculation applies the effect of the trial cited: A quarter (cautious), half (central), all of it (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20% of water, with no yield gain.

10.4 Importing water-retention polymers into Egypt

What to know

• Customs duty (heading 3906.90, acrylic polymers in primary forms): The rate was not found in an official source (A12).

• Import VAT: 14%, calculated on the CIF value plus customs duty (A12).

• Registration: A fertiliser and soil conditioner control department exists at the Soil, Water and Environment Research Institute, and the Egyptian standards body publishes standards on soil conditioners (A13). The exact registration procedure was not found on an official site. No text specific to water-retaining agents was found.

• Classification: Code 3906.90 is an inference for our products; Egyptian Customs settle it.

Annex B. Calculation method

1. Agricultural water. Total withdrawals (77,500 Mm³, 2022) × agricultural share (79.16%) = 61,350.0 Mm³.

2. Irrigation. The equipped area (4,013,710 ha) and agricultural water are shared between irrigated groups (wheat, barley, sorghum, maize, rice, sugar cane and sugar beet, potato, vegetables and melons, citrus, date palm, other fruit, cotton, groundnut, sesame, sunflower, soybean and pulses) in proportion to their harvested areas (5,876,526 ha), without exceeding each group's harvested area. Our sources have no irrigation statistic by crop. Other crops are treated as rainfed and receive no irrigation water in the calculation. The equipped area is smaller than the sum of harvested areas of the irrigable crops, because several crops follow each other on the same land: Agricultural water is therefore shared pro rata to harvested areas, and the irrigated area attributed to each group is capped at its harvested area. Wheat, rice, maize and cane are treated as irrigated, Egyptian farming being almost entirely irrigated.

3. Scenarios. Water saved = group water × trial effect: A quarter (cautious), half (central), all of it (high). Without a trial: 5, 10 and 20%.

4. Extra hectares. Water saved ÷ water per equipped hectare (15,285 m³/ha).

6. Value of water. Three assumed prices (0.10; 0.22 and 0.45 $/m³); no national tariff was adopted.

7. Fertilisers. Minimum spending = nitrogen ÷ 0.46 × urea price + phosphate ÷ 0.46 × TSP price + potash ÷ 0.60 × potassium chloride price (A7).

8. Climate. Rainfall, reference evapotranspiration (FAO-56) and temperature: 1991-2020 means of the ERA5 grid cell containing each city; radiation: PVGIS, 2005-2020. Temperature trends are linear regressions over 1981-2024. ERA5 is a reanalysis: Its rainfall can differ from local station records. On the maps, each broad area is shown as a point at its reference town: The twenty-seven governorates are too small for a readable map.

9. Trade. Totals are those of the FAOSTAT crops and livestock line; products are listed one by one, without aggregates. Rice amounts add husked rice, broken rice and milled rice.
